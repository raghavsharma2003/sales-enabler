# Voice-AI vendor terms: what a competitor may do on a trial account

Read 2026-10-20 from public pages only. Vendors: Bolna, Retell AI, Sarvam AI, Vapi, ElevenLabs, Exotel. Machine-readable twin: `terms.json`.

> **This is a reading of the vendors' published terms, not legal advice.** It records what the words say and where they are unclear. The verdicts are not a legal opinion, and none of this has been reviewed by a lawyer. A signed order form, MSA or written consent from the vendor overrides the click-through terms read here.

## The question

VP is a startup building its own voice-agent platform. It wants a trial account at each vendor, used as a customer, to evaluate the product. For each vendor this file sets out what the current public terms say on six topics, with exact quotes. It then classifies five activities:

- (a) Normal customer use via UI and API (build agent, synthetic contacts, test calls to own phones, time steps, read analytics)
- (b) Same as (a) but driven by scripted browser automation (Playwright)
- (c) Inspecting the logged-in app's network traffic or JS bundles / source maps
- (d) Using what we learn to design our own competing product (patterns, not code/UI)
- (e) Keeping recordings / transcripts of our own test calls

**How verdicts are assigned.** *Allowed*: an express grant covers the activity and no restriction's words reach it. *Prohibited*: a restriction's words describe the activity directly. *Ambiguous*: the words could reach it without naming it, the deciding document is missing, or the answer turns on a fact the terms do not settle. Verdicts take the stated purpose as given (a competitor evaluating in order to inform its own product), because several clauses turn on purpose rather than mechanics.

## What stands out

1. **Five of six bar competitive use; Vapi is the exception.** The clauses differ in how far they reach. Retell (§10.1(d) "benchmarking or competitive analysis"), ElevenLabs (PUP §12 "research or develop products or services that compete with ours") and Sarvam (EULA (vi) "direct competitor ... for benchmarking or competitive purposes") bar the evaluation itself. Bolna bars "use the Platform to develop a competing product". Exotel's §6.2 bars building a competing product at all, whatever use is made of the trial.
2. **Plain hands-on evaluation (a) is clearly allowed only at Vapi.** At Retell, ElevenLabs and Sarvam it is prohibited unless the vendor consents in writing. At Bolna and Exotel the wording does not settle it.
3. **Playwright (b) is barred wherever automation is addressed**: Vapi item 8, Retell §10.1(b), ElevenLabs PUP §12, Sarvam §9/§10.5(e). ElevenLabs' robots.txt exception does not help, because robots.txt disallows `/app/` for all user agents. Bolna and Exotel say nothing. Bolna incorporates an Acceptable Use Policy that is not published anywhere.
4. **Inspecting traffic, bundles or source maps (c) is barred by five of the six as reverse engineering.** Sarvam's §10.5(b) is the only clause that names "monitoring or tracking the inputs and outputs flowing through the Offerings". Exotel has no reverse-engineering clause, but saving its UI code is close to §6.2's "copy ... download ... Proprietary Technology", and its confidentiality clause covers non-public product information.
5. **Keeping recordings (e) is mostly fine; using them is not.** Vapi, ElevenLabs and Sarvam say you own the Output, Retell licenses it to you for internal business purposes, Exotel lets you download it, and Bolna is silent. But Vapi (datasets for third-party products), Retell (competitive analysis; training competing models), ElevenLabs (improving any AI service) and Sarvam (anything derived from the Offerings) each restrict what the recordings may feed.
6. **They may train on what you put in.** Retell records calls and trains on them unless you opt out (§11). ElevenLabs takes a perpetual, irrevocable licence to your Content to improve the Services and develop new products, and an opt-out applies only going forward (§4(d), §4(i)). Sarvam's Privacy Policy text trains unless you opt out, which contradicts its own "Opt-In" heading and the EULA. Vapi's ToS limits itself to de-identified service improvement, while its 2023 Privacy Policy is broader. Bolna "may review call content". In practice, VP's own prompts and persona design go into a competitor's platform with these licences attached. Opt out before the first call, and use throwaway prompts.
7. **Your identity must be real.** Five vendors require accurate registration information: Bolna §1, Vapi §3(a) (and item 12, no misrepresenting your affiliation), Sarvam §3.1, ElevenLabs §2 and Exotel §7, whose KYC asks for the incorporation certificate. Retell's ToS has no general accuracy clause, but its PUP §11 says you must describe your use case "accurately and promptly" if asked. Evaluating under a cover identity would be a separate breach on top of whatever the evaluation itself breaches.
8. **ElevenLabs Starter changes one thing.** It moves you from the non-commercial Free bar (ToU §1(c)(i)) to commercial use. The PUP §12 ban on competitive research, automation and reverse engineering applies on every plan.
9. **There are written-permission routes.** Retell §10.1(d) ("prior written consent"), Sarvam §10.5(a) ("except as expressly permitted by us in writing") and ElevenLabs ToU §5(b) ("prior written permission") allow written consent. A signed agreement takes precedence over the click-through terms at Vapi (§20), Bolna (§12) and Exotel (preamble).
10. **Exotel's clause matters beyond this teardown.** §6.2 attaches to being a customer, not to the evaluation. If VP intends to buy Exotel telephony for its own platform, §6.2 on its face applies whenever VP competes with Exotel's Product. The way out the terms themselves offer is a separate contract (preamble).
11. **The automated-access wording also reaches public websites.** Vapi item 8 names "our website", and Sarvam's terms define the Website as part of the Offerings. Scripted fetching of their public pages is therefore within the words too, which matters for the radar probe scripts in `../radar/code/`. This reading itself fetched each legal page once with curl.

## Method

- Each page was fetched with `curl` (through the session proxy, no cookies, no sign-in, no forms) and parsed from server-rendered HTML to text. All six vendors serve their legal text in the HTML, so no JS rendering, cache or llms.txt fallback was needed. Bolna's and Sarvam's llms.txt and sitemaps were read only to look for further legal pages.
- Every quote below was checked by script against the fetched text: 167 fragments, 0 misses, with whitespace normalised. A ` … ` inside a quote joins non-adjacent sentences of the same document, and each fragment was checked separately. The section labels are this file's, taken from each document's headings. Vapi's §2(d) list is unnumbered on the page, so the item numbers are its on-page order.
- "Not addressed" means the documents listed for that vendor contain no clause on the topic. Nothing was inferred to fill the gap.
- **Not verified.** In-app click-through text shown at sign-up, order forms, DPAs (Vapi, ElevenLabs and Sarvam reference one), and anything behind a login were not read. Bolna's Acceptable Use Policy is referenced but not published. Exotel's page shows no date; the date given is from page metadata.
