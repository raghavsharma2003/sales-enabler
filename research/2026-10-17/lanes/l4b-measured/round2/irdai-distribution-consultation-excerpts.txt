=== Secondary cross-check: Khaitan & Co ERGO note, 29 Sep 2026 (key takeaways, burden) ===
The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) has released a two-part public
consultation paper titled ‘Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution’ (Consultation Paper). Part 1
sets out the proposed reforms. Part 2 presents IRDAI’s analysis of distribution costs and commission
structures across the industry. Together, they propose the most significant overhaul of the Indian insurance
regime. Comments are due by 25 October 2026.

Key Takeaways

WHAT IRDAI PROPOSES

• One licence for entities; one framework for individuals. 8 forms of distribution licence would be
  subsumed within a single Insurance Distribution Entity (IDE) licence, and nine forms of authorised
  persons into four categories of Insurance Distribution Persons (IDPs). Every IDE would have the option
  of open architecture (i.e. distribute products of multiple insurers).

• Lower Expenses of Management (EoM) limits. A glide path from FY 2027-28: life insurers to 15% of
  premium within 2 years and 12.5% within 5 years; general insurers to 25% and 20%. The EoM base changes
  and the 2023 carve-outs are absorbed within the overall limit.

• Return of commission caps. Product-level caps, which sit well below current market levels, with lower
  caps for IDEs (eligible for open architecture) than for agents and associates engaged directly by insurers.

• Conduct measures. Clawback, a persistency-linked renewal commission escalator, enforceable suitability
  and seller-level accountability, with incidences of mis-selling placed in the public domain.

• New market infrastructure. Insurer-promoted market infrastructure institutions (MIIs), with Bima Sugam
  as the first, and an IRDAI-promoted Public Insurance Registry (PIR).

OUR VIEW

• IRDAI’s diagnosis is well evidenced; however, the remedy is more contestable. The suggested distribution
  caps are a blunt instrument as they regulate the quantum paid, and not the conduct it rewards. The
  conduct-focused measures are the most consequential proposals and, if strengthened, could deliver
  durable change without the distortions that caps invite.

• The reforms ask distributors to sell better, through documented needs analysis, suitability, verified
  customer contact and sustained servicing, and each of these adds cost. The caps compress the very
  remuneration that pays for that effort. Unless the framework leaves room to reward quality, for example
  through persistency- and suitability-linked pay above the base cap, distributors may cut the advisory
  effort the reforms rely on rather than the cost the caps target.

• The burden will not be borne evenly. It falls hardest on NBFC-led credit life, Point of Sale Person (PoSP)
  led retail broking, OEM-linked motor distribution and employee benefits broking. Sub-scale insurers face
  the steepest adjustment, and consolidation is a likely consequence if the reforms are carried forward.
