# Bolna product research — India-focused voice operations

**Research date:** 22 September 2026  
**Scope and method:** Public, first-party Bolna website and documentation only; 16 substantive pages reviewed. This is a product/UX reading, not an authenticated-platform trial: UI labels and screenshots below are vendor-published documentation, and no account, purchase, phone number, or call was created. Product claims, availability, prices, and provider coverage should be reconfirmed in a sales/pilot evaluation.

## Executive read

Bolna presents as an India-focused voice-AI orchestration layer, rather than merely a voice model. Its clear product story is: create a conversational/graph agent, choose the speech, LLM, TTS and carrier layers, attach business data and actions, then operate inbound, one-off outbound, or scheduled bulk calling from one workspace/API. The strongest differentiators for an Indian business are its explicitly documented live Indian-language switching, India carrier options and regulated-number workflow, and an unusually operational surface: retries, calling-hour restrictions, CRM-oriented webhooks, transcripts/traces and per-call cost data.

The platform’s public claims are ambitious—vernacular/cross-language calling, low latency, a multi-provider stack, and India/US residency/on-prem deployment. Treat these as supplier assertions, not independently benchmarked guarantees. In particular, the documentation does not by itself prove end-to-end latency, recognition quality for a particular accent/noise condition, or whether every third-party model can remain in India.

## The operator journey: create → configure → test → deploy → improve

1. **Create.** A team can choose a template/blank agent, or use **Agent Studio**. The latter asks for identity/goal, conversation, and closing; it accepts PDF, DOCX, TXT or CSV business material, generates a structured prompt and auto-selects a voice. Bolna says generation takes 5–8 minutes, then the editable agent can be tested in-browser or with a test call. The required fields include one or more languages (one primary), gender, tone, role and a 1–2 sentence call outcome; the next step explicitly captures welcome text, guardrails, numbered conversation flow and a close. This is a good first-run pattern because it turns business operating rules into reviewable inputs rather than hiding them in one generic prompt. [Agent Studio](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/agent-setup/studio)

2. **Configure intelligence, voice and language deliberately.** The Intelligence tab selects the provider/model, output-token limit, temperature/reasoning effort, and one or more knowledge bases. Connected provider keys make model selection an economic and governance decision as well as a quality decision.

   In the Languages tab, every configured language can have its own STT provider, TTS provider/model, voice and hand-in message. The published selector includes AzureTTS, Cartesia, ElevenLabs and Sarvam; voice preview and tunable buffer, speed, stability and style make voice QA a concrete pre-launch task. Higher buffering may smooth audio but increases delay. [Languages, voice and transcription](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/agent-setup/audio-tab)

   **Hinglish/code-switching and transcription:** Bolna’s public product page names Hinglish, Hindi, Tamil and Telugu among its target Indian languages. More specifically, its language-switching document says a multilingual agent runs one call across configured languages: recognition, configured voice and replies switch together mid-call. The default compares a recognizer fixed to the current language with a background detector, and is intentionally conservative: a full Hindi sentence can switch; one embedded English word, greetings, OTPs and order numbers should not. Auto-detection is documented for English plus Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Tamil, Gujarati, Telugu, Punjabi, Kannada, Urdu and Malayalam. This handles the *language switching* part of Hinglish; it should not be read as a validated claim that every provider transcribes arbitrary code-mixed speech equally well. Run a call-set evaluation with regional accents, mixed sentences, named entities, IDs and noisy mobiles. [Language switching behavior](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/customizations/language-switching-behavior)

3. **Make turn-taking and call conditions explicit.** Engine settings expose “precise transcript,” interruption-word threshold, endpointing, linear delay and caller-presence detection. The docs say stopwords such as “Stop,” “Wait,” and “Hold On” pause immediately, while the word threshold avoids reacting to short speech; their starting guidance is 200–300 ms endpointing and 400–500 ms linear delay. This is a valuable calibration surface, but the right setup needs test calls because lower latency can cut callers off. [Latency and interruptions](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/agent-setup/engine-tab)

   The Call tab adds carrier choice, adjustable background-noise cancellation, voicemail detection, DTMF, retry/reschedule and outbound local-time windows. Ambient *background* tracks are a separate feature (coffee shop, office, call centre, or custom WAV/MP3) and are documented as available only with Plivo and Vobiz; do not conflate that with noise cancellation. It defaults off. Its 9am–9pm example is illustrative; restrictions are off by default and should be set to policy/compliance requirements. [Call configuration](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/agent-setup/call-tab)

4. **Attach knowledge and actions.** Knowledge bases are RAG over PDF and URL sources, then must be attached in the Intelligence tab. Bolna supports an English default or a multilingual mode for Hindi/Tamil/etc. and says it enables mixed-language/cross-lingual retrieval. KB processing status must be `processed`; existing English/default KBs cannot simply be toggled to multilingual. That last constraint is worth exposing during content operations. [Knowledge base](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/knowledge-base)

   The Tools tab contains Cal.com availability/booking, live transfer to a human or another number (optional pre-call webhook), and a generic external API tool defined manually as JSON or generated from cURL. This is the credible “human-in-the-loop” and CRM/line-of-business integration path—not a claim of a built-in CRM. [Tools and API integrations](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/agent-setup/tools-tab)

5. **Deploy on the appropriate channel.** For inbound, map an available number to an agent and optionally identify callers/preload context from internal API, CSV or Google Sheet. The API input includes caller number, agent ID and execution ID; returned JSON is injected into the prompt. This enables CRM-like personalization but raises a prompt-injection/data-minimization review requirement. [Inbound and caller matching](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/agent-setup/inbound-tab)

   For outbound scale, batches upload a CSV using E.164 `contact_number` plus row-level variables, pick a managed/BYOC origin number, run now or schedule, and configure up to three retries by outcome. A per-batch webhook receives execution status, transcript, extracted data and cost; results can otherwise be retrieved through the batch API. [Batch calling](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/outbound/batch-calling)

   **WhatsApp and CRM follow-up:** Bolna’s beta Workflows can combine calls, API calls, waits/retries, CRM pushes and AiSensy WhatsApp-template nodes in a published-version graph; a campaign executes it independently for each contact. This is a stronger cross-channel pattern than a simple batch, but beta status and the named AiSensy dependency should be treated as rollout constraints. [Workflows introduction](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/workflows/introduction)

   Bolna’s official integrations directory names Exotel, Plivo and Vobiz for Indian inbound/outbound calling, Twilio for global coverage, and SIP/BYOC including Airtel SIP. Its compliance guide says a one-time application precedes number purchase and requests business-identifying details, including CIN documentation. The operational implication: phone provisioning and campaign start are not frictionless “click to call”; let compliance, carrier/KYC, DLT/regulated-number requirements and consent policy lead the rollout plan. [Telephony integrations](https://www.bolna.ai/integrations/telephony) · [India number compliance](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/compliance-application/introduction)

6. **Observe and learn.** The Extractions tab sends real-time execution data to a webhook, adds automatic summaries, and derives structured fields such as lead quality, appointments, sentiment and handover signal—explicitly positioned for CRM updates. The call history reports attempts, status, cost and duration; individual calls provide recordings, transcripts, raw JSON and component/provider traces. This is sufficient for a disciplined test-and-improve loop, provided the buyer defines retention/access controls before recording customer calls. [Webhooks and post-call extraction](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/agent-setup/analytics-tab) · [Conversations, metrics and logs](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/agent-setup/call-history)

## India, enterprise and cost: evidence with limits

**Residency.** Bolna states that services default to US-hosted infrastructure. It says Enterprise customers may select India residency, where customer audio, transcripts, logs and configurations are physically stored in India and inference/transcription/response generation occur within India. This is a material claim, but the public page does not state certification, subprocessors, encryption/key management, deletion/retention terms, availability SLA, or the treatment of every connected provider. Ask for contractual DPA, data-flow diagram and provider-by-provider India-processing confirmation before treating this as a regulated deployment answer. [India data residency](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/enterprise/data-residency)

**Cost.** The documentation describes three cost components: STT+LLM+TTS usage, carrier minutes, and a Bolna platform fee of $0.02/minute. It says BYOK for telephony/transcription/LLM/TTS transfers those component costs and can leave the platform fee, while the dashboard provides an approximate estimate and execution history shows consumption. Calculate an India pilot TCO using the exact carrier, model, pulse/billing rules, concurrency and retry rate—not the headline platform fee. [Call pricing](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/pricing/call-pricing)

## Comparative product takeaways for an Indian business system

| Decision | Takeaway |
| --- | --- |
| **Adopt** | A layered configuration model: business role/guardrails → per-language voice pipeline → business tools/KB → channel → post-call data. It makes QA and ownership clear. |
| **Adopt** | Conservative live language switching with specific tests for full switch, borrowed-word Hinglish, OTP/order IDs and related languages. This is far more operational than a generic “multilingual” badge. |
| **Adapt** | The batch control plane: E.164 validation, row variables, scheduled launch, outcome-specific retry, local-time protection, callback/webhook, export and per-call trace. Add explicit consent, DNC, frequency caps and approval gates for the business’s risk model. |
| **Adapt** | Human transfer plus structured handover extraction. Require an availability/queue strategy and surface the context sent to the human, rather than merely exposing a transfer button. |
| **Avoid** | Treating provider pickers, <300 ms marketing latency, “human-like” voice, Hinglish support, India residency or volume claims as proof of production fit. Benchmark the chosen India carrier/STT/TTS/model combination with the actual call list, accents, noise and CRM workflow. |
