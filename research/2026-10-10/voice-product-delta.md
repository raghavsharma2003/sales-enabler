# Voice product operator-flow delta — 10 October 2026

**Scope.** This bounded review uses official public sources and focuses on what an operator can configure, test, hear, approve, and release. A documented control is a verified public capability; naturalness, latency, accuracy, and business outcomes remain vendor claims unless the underlying test is public. No accounts, calls, uploads, or paid/provider actions were used.

## The concrete operator flows

### Vapi: the most complete “configure → replay → canary” reference

An operator edits an assistant draft, chooses voice/transcriber/model, tunes start- and stop-speaking plans, then publishes a named immutable version. Voice speed is not a universal Vapi control: the speech guide says only PlayHT currently honors `speed`; other provider-specific voice objects expose different controls. Endpointing and interruption are first-class: the dashboard exposes wait, punctuation/no-punctuation/number delays, interruption word/voice thresholds, and resume backoff ([speech configuration](https://docs.vapi.ai/customization/speech-configuration), [versioning](https://docs.vapi.ai/assistants/versioning/versioning-assistants)).

The evidence ladder is unusually explicit. Evals test a next-turn decision; chat Simulations test whole outcomes cheaply; voice Simulations add synthetic speech, transcription, pacing, and turn-taking. A voice run carries a transcript and recording, but success criteria do **not** grade pronunciation, pauses, or interruptions, so Vapi tells the operator to listen to recordings and use manual/phone calls for noise, accents, silence, and carrier behavior ([testing guide](https://docs.vapi.ai/test/voice-testing), [simulation result flow](https://docs.vapi.ai/observability/simulations-quickstart)). Reusable scenarios, repeated iterations, required/informational latency limits, tool mocks, and explicit skipped/unmeasurable states make the run evidence reproducible.

After tests, the operator can publish at 0%, 100%, or open a beta traffic editor. Splits target up to five published versions, total exactly 100%, keep best-effort caller stickiness, retain allocation history, and support optimistic concurrency. This is controlled rollout, not a built-in blind perceptual test: Vapi explicitly lacks per-version metrics and side-by-side comparison in the beta, so operators must join calls by `assistantVersion` themselves ([traffic splitting](https://docs.vapi.ai/assistants/versioning/traffic-splitting)). Cost remains uncertain until execution: the model picker is an estimate, while completed call logs may show actual component cost ([cost model](https://docs.vapi.ai/assistants/model-intelligence/understanding-cost)).

### ElevenLabs: strongest experiment and review workflow

In ElevenAgents, the operator selects a voice and model, previews pronunciations, and sets speed from 0.7× to 1.2× where supported. Stability, similarity, style, pronunciation, and expressive controls are model-dependent. The TTS docs say generation is nondeterministic and extreme settings can hurt quality ([agent voice customization](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/customization/voice), [TTS settings](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/playground/text-to-speech)).

Tests can be created from a failed conversation, then run as next-reply, tool-call, or multi-turn simulation cases. Repeating a case 3×, 5×, or 15× yields pass rates and clusters failure reasons, but the judge evaluates transcript behavior; it is not a listening panel ([agent testing](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/customization/agent-testing)). For release, a branch can receive 5–10% of live traffic, with deterministic routing by conversation ID, branch-filtered analytics, and promotion by increasing traffic or merging. Voice selection, model, and speed can all be experiment variables ([experiments](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/operate/experiments)).

Merge proposals add human review of diffs, test pass rates, and recent conversations; one non-author approval is normally required, though an admin can bypass it. Required pre-merge tests are still planned, so the product does not yet prove “eval passed before release” by construction ([merge proposals](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/operate/merge-proposals)). Calls are duration-billed, long silences receive a discount, and LLM cost is separate; displayed estimates are based on past behavior, not a fixed quote ([Agents cost](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/eleven-agents/how-much-does-eleven-agents-cost)).

### Retell: clearest operational QA, but its simulations are text

Retell’s practical sequence is draft version → Test LLM/simulation → Test Audio web call → real phone test → publish → move `staging`/`prod` tag. Published versions are read-only and tags can move back for rollback ([testing overview](https://docs.retellai.com/test/test-overview), [versions and tags](https://docs.retellai.com/agent/version)). Web calls use real audio and let an operator hear pacing, pronunciation, latency, and interruption behavior; they can start at a selected flow node and mock functions, but transfers and carrier behavior require a phone test ([web-call testing](https://docs.retellai.com/test/test-web)).

Production A/B is a phone-number toggle: bind agents, set weights totalling 100%, then deploy. It compares voices, prompts, or flows, but the docs do not claim blinding or automatic statistical promotion ([A/B testing](https://docs.retellai.com/deploy/ab-testing)). AI QA samples calls, applies weighted AI conditions plus hard metrics, then lets an operator replay failures with transcript-linked evidence. This is strong triage, not human ground truth ([AI QA](https://www.retellai.com/features/ai-qa)). Testing is billable at production rates across messages, grading, and voice minutes ([testing pricing](https://docs.retellai.com/test/testing-pricing)).

### Bolna: excellent tuning and replay diagnostics, weak automated release gate

Bolna’s Languages tab configures each language independently: provider → model → voice → preview of the welcome prompt. Advanced controls include buffer size, speed rate, similarity, stability, and style where the provider supports them. Its Engine tab separately tunes interruption word count, endpointing, linear delay, silence re-engagement, and final-message interruptibility ([voice configuration](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/agent-setup/audio-tab), [engine controls](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/agent-setup/engine-tab)).

After a real execution, Call History provides waveform playback/download, transcript, config and cost breakdown, component and P90 latency, interrupted-speech markers, extraction reasoning, execution logs, and raw JSON. Operators can rate a call, but the public docs do not show blind comparison, a reusable audio simulation suite, or a pass that blocks release ([call history](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/agent-setup/call-history)). Its readiness checklist instead requires at least 20 production-configuration test calls, manual same-day review of a small first campaign, and cost measured on real traffic ([readiness checklist](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/production/readiness-checklist)). Published pricing starts from a flat/volume minute rate, while provider/BYOK composition and telephony can change actual spend ([pricing](https://www.bolna.ai/pricing)).

### Tavus/Griffin: valuable human-preview boundary, not yet a deployable Griffin benchmark

Griffin-Lite is a research preview for selected testers, not a generally documented operator surface. Tavus reports its own full-duplex video benchmark and a study in which 48% of participants believed the system was human; those are first-party research claims, not evidence Relay can configure or reproduce today ([Griffin](https://www.tavus.io/griffin)).

The shipping PAL platform is more concrete. TTS can pin Tavus, ElevenLabs, Cartesia, or limited Azure voices; ElevenLabs speed/stability/style and Cartesia global or per-phrase SSML controls are documented. `tavus-auto` may change underlying providers, so pinning is required for reproducibility ([PAL TTS](https://docs.tavus.io/sections/conversational-video-interface/pal/tts)). Tavus’s build-and-verify loop publishes a PAL, generates adversarial probes, runs text-only CVI turns, judges objectives/guardrails/tools, then hands a full preview URL to a human for audio/video QA. It explicitly says text verification does not validate audio latency or rendering ([build and verify](https://docs.tavus.io/sections/agent-tools/pal-build-and-verify)). The release weakness is material: Maker drafts exist only after deployment, API PATCH writes directly to the live PAL, and there is no public immutable version/rollback model ([editing lifecycle](https://docs.tavus.io/sections/conversational-video-interface/pal/editing-lifecycle)). Recordings can land in customer S3/GCS/Azure with a ready webhook, which is artifact delivery rather than a scored replay suite ([recordings](https://docs.tavus.io/sections/conversational-video-interface/quickstart/conversation-recordings)).

### Dialflo and Alchemic: service-shaped comparisons

Dialflo exposes prompt, `voice_id`, version number, call context, task ID, outcome, recording/transcript references, and completion webhook; agent setup and webhook activation still tell the buyer to contact the team. Its public docs recommend a moderate pace and “test different voices” but expose no speed/prosody schema, repeatable eval suite, blind A/B, human QA queue, or eval-to-release binding ([quickstart](https://docs.dialflo.ai/quickstart), [voice guidance](https://docs.dialflo.ai/voice_call)). The current commercial claim is ₹8 or ₹5 per **resolved** call, but “resolved” is vendor-defined and the sample webhook infers satisfaction from cooperation; Relay should require outcome receipts and dispute handling ([pricing](https://www.dialflo.ai/), [webhook example](https://docs.dialflo.ai/api-reference/webhook)).

Alchemic is a managed research workflow: brief discussion guide/screener/quotas/language routing → calls with consent → live themes → theme → respondent → quote → exact audio moment. Human researchers tailor the moderator, and concept studies support monadic cells (each respondent sees one variant) or randomized sequential monadic designs. That is the closest public reference to bias-reduced comparison, but it compares concepts with recruited respondents, not synthetic-voice candidates or agent releases ([phone research](https://thealchemic.com/ai-phone-research/), [concept testing](https://thealchemic.com/concept-testing/)). Pricing is scoped per study and sample source, so no unit cost can be inferred publicly ([pricing model](https://thealchemic.com/feeds/market-research-cost-pricing-models)).

## Top three build decisions for the current platform

1. **Turn the existing 9.7-second artifact into a reviewable voice evidence row.** Preserve its exact agent/runtime/config version, fixture digest, generated-audio digest, duration, trace, latency proxy, and actual/estimated/unknown cost. Add one operator action: play the artifact and record `pass`, `fail`, or `not reviewed` against a short rubric for intelligibility, pronunciation, pace, clipping, and unwanted silence. Until that happens, keep the current truth: “audio generated and playable,” never “voice quality passed.”

2. **Add paired blind listening before adding broad provider choice.** Generate candidate A/B artifacts from the same approved English fixture, normalize presentation, hide provider/setting labels and order, and ask at least two reviewers for preference plus defect tags. Store each candidate’s concealed configuration and reveal it only after verdict submission. The current single fixture can validate the mechanism; it cannot establish production voice quality, Indian-language performance, or statistical superiority.

3. **Bind evidence to release with explicit uncertainty.** A candidate version may advance only when required protocol checks pass and the configured voice gate is either human-passed or visibly waived by an authorized operator. Carry separate states for `text passed`, `audio generated`, `listening pending`, `listening failed`, and `provider outcome uncertain`; never collapse them into green. Publish the packet with actual cost when known, an upper-bound/estimate label otherwise, deployment target, reviewer, waiver reason, and rollback version. This adapts Vapi’s versioned canary, ElevenLabs’ review packet, Retell’s replay QA, and Tavus’s human preview without importing their provider spend or live-traffic risk.
