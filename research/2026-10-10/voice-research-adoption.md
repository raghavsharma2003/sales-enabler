# Voice research adoption — 10 October 2026

**Decision:** implement a versioned, evaluation-only Azure acoustic delivery profile and a paired listening evidence contract. Keep the current voice and provider as the control. The next experiment should change only output speed, initially **1.0 versus 0.9**, and measure what was captured and heard. These values are proposed experimental conditions, not a research-backed optimum. A slower setting cannot establish warmth, empathy, Hindi quality, pronunciation accuracy or full-duplex intelligence by itself.

This review extends [the October 9 frontier review](../2026-10-09/voice-frontier-papers.md) and [voice quality program](../../docs/voice-quality-program-oct09.md). It adds seven important developments or material clarifications rather than repeating the prior model catalog. Research checked on October 10; publication/release dates below are separate from the check date. Only original papers, author projects, official repositories/model cards and Microsoft documentation support the conclusions. Published scores were not reproduced. No paid inference, model download, dependency install, Docker action, deployment, application edit or context edit occurred.

## Ranked decisions

| Rank | Development / contribution | Implement now | Experiment later | Reject for this product now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CtrlSpeech: independently measurable acoustic controls | Separate requested and observed pace; preserve unsupported controls explicitly | Offline pitch/duration research only after appropriate rights are established | Shipping its noncommercial weights; claiming session speed gives phoneme-level control |
| 2 | Kyutai/Gradium interaction alignment | Joint timing, semantic-completion and interruption rubrics | A frozen English duplex reference arm if a commercially usable checkpoint is selected | The released Seamless RL checkpoints as SaaS weights; optimizing response delay alone |

The ranks prioritize changes useful to this repository, not a universal model leaderboard. The research descriptions and licenses supporting each row follow.

## 1. CtrlSpeech — precise controls are a different capability from expressive prompting

**August 8, 2026 preprint; project labels Interspeech 2026.** Zhisheng Zheng and David Harwath at UT Austin, with Xiaohang Sun, Zhu Liu, Caren Chen, Rohith Kumar, Manoj Aggarwal and Gerard Medioni at Amazon, separate speaker conditioning from phone-aligned pitch, loudness and duration. A DiTAR-derived autoregressive/diffusion model supports local prosody edits. The paper explicitly limits evidence to predominantly English speech; code-switching is unexplored, alignment errors matter, and emotion/nonverbal vocalizations are not explicit controls. Its strongest product lesson is to compare the requested acoustic attribute with the achieved one, rather than score an instruction as success. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08362v1), [author project and affiliations](https://zhishengzheng.com/ctrlspeech/).

**Available, but not a clean commercial model option.** The [repository](https://github.com/zszheng147/ctrlspeech) includes synthesis/editing code; its [MIT license](https://github.com/zszheng147/ctrlspeech/blob/main/LICENSE) covers source only. The [weights](https://huggingface.co/zhisheng01/CtrlSpeech) are CC BY-NC 4.0, English, with 150M/692M controlled variants. Duration editing requires an English Montreal Forced Aligner setup. This is an offline synthesis/refinement reference, not evidence of streaming conversational latency. For our Azure adapter, record pitch/emphasis/phoneme support as unavailable rather than translating them into unverified API fields.

## 2. Multi-Faceted Interactivity Alignment — a more useful Moshi development than raw frame latency

**June 9, 2026 paper; June 10 lab announcement.** Atsumoto Ohashi, Neil Zeghidour, Alexandre Défossez and Eugene Kharitonov span Kyutai and Gradium. They apply GRPO with separate pause, turn-taking, backchannel and interruption rewards, plus an LLM semantic-quality reward, to Moshi and PersonaPlex. Static prerecorded-input and interactive multi-turn evaluations are distinct. Ablations show that removing pause examples encourages premature speaking, while removing turn examples produces overly conservative timing. Removing the semantic reward damages response quality. Human interaction evaluation remains future work, and the paper documents safety degradation in a PersonaPlex RL case. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11167v1), [dated announcement](https://kyutai.org/blog/2026-06-10-interactivity/).

**Important license delta:** the released [Moshika RL weights](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/moshika-rl-seamless) are CC BY-NC 4.0. The [PersonaPlex RL weights](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/personaplex-rl-seamless) require both CC BY-NC 4.0 and NVIDIA Open Model terms; the original Moshi lineage is CC BY 4.0. Both are English research releases. Do not transfer the base model's commercial status to these derivatives. Their four-axis evaluation design is immediately useful without installing the models.

For this application, every faster-turn result must also report premature responses, incomplete answers, critical-entity accuracy and successful repair. A listener backchannel and a takeover must be separate conditions, even if both trigger speech activity.

## 3. PERSIST / SpokenTrace — the genuinely new October memory result

**October 6, 2026.** Achira Lin, Siyuan Hou, Wenyi Yu, Xinnian Zhao and colleagues, with Chao Zhang, at Tsinghua University and Huawei, combine semantic content, speaker grounding and temporal state in retrieval. The SALMONN-omni implementation reuses cached internal representations. Reported retrieval time falls from **578.42 to 7.03 ms**; that is not time to first spoken answer. SpokenTrace evaluates 1,200 episodes with current-only and missing-evidence controls; the reported 85.08% strict answer accuracy uses a model judge. Histories and query speech are constructed/generated, so this is not longitudinal evidence from real company customers. [Original paper, including benchmark and implementation appendices](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07725v1).

**Availability boundary:** the paper states a benchmark release, but this review did not verify an official downloadable repository/checkpoint and governing artifact license. The paper's arXiv distribution license does not grant model rights. Internal encoder states are unavailable through the present Azure API.

**Adopt the problem definition:** preserve evidence of both an earlier preference and its correction, distinguish speaker from subject, and abstain when the authorized contact scope lacks support. Keep authenticated/contact-linked identity authoritative. Acoustic similarity must not link anonymous callers to customer records or authorize disclosure. Our application can test these behaviors on CPU with synthetic records; it does not need PERSIST's speaker embeddings or backbone surgery.

## 4. MoshiRAG — asynchronous information arrival is feasible; internal model injection is not an Azure adapter switch

**April 14, 2026.** Chung-Ming Chien and Karen Livescu at TTIC, with Manu Orsini, Eugene Kharitonov, Neil Zeghidour and Alexandre Défossez at Kyutai/Gradium, combine a compact duplex interface with asynchronous retrieval. A learned trigger initiates retrieval; a reference encoder injects returned information while the speech model continues. Streaming ASR supplies the retrieval context. The study separates beginning a response from delivering its substantive information, improving factuality without requiring a larger always-active speech model. That distinction is useful, but a quick acknowledgment cannot be counted as a completed answer. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12928v1).

The [official repository](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/moshi-rag) provides Python/Rust inference and BF16 Moshika checkpoints: Python/client MIT, Rust Apache-2.0, weights CC BY 4.0. This is a released systems comparison, not merely a proposal. Azure-only use would require Azure-hosted model compute and a separately approved retrieval backend; its external backend examples are not authorized integrations here.

**Application adaptation:** tag any retrieval with conversation, turn and generation IDs, cancel or mark it obsolete after a correction, and require source validation before factual speech/tool action. The existing Azure route can adopt that lifecycle. It cannot reproduce trained streaming reference injection by appending arbitrary mid-response instructions.

## 5. RHELM — evaluate relationship continuity against changing company sources

**May 29, 2026 paper, revised June 1; repository reports EMNLP Findings acceptance August 30.** Han Zhang, Zihao Tang, Xin Yu and colleagues, with Hanfang Yang, at Renmin University and Microsoft, construct evolving personas with a plan/rollout/evolve/prune process and synchronized dialogue, email and document sources. This is relevant to company operations because a saved preference may conflict with a newer document or a later explicit correction. It evaluates memory and reasoning, not vocal empathy. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31086), [project and author affiliations](https://microsoft.github.io/RHELM/).

The [code](https://github.com/microsoft/RHELM) is MIT; the [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/microsoft/RHELM) is CC BY 4.0 and fully synthetic English text, with 1,305 QA pairs. The live project page and dataset card disagree on some corpus counts and 26 versus 27 challenge characteristics. Pin a dataset revision and count local records before any reproduction claim.

**Adopt:** a small company fixture set where the same answer requires correct source, person, time and explicit correction. Score missing-evidence abstention and source conflict separately from factual recall. Keep memory-correctness results separate from how warm the answer sounds. Full benchmark execution with model judging is later work, not needed to define the first acoustic experiment.

## 6. PersonaPlex — released full-duplex baseline with an easily misquoted checkpoint

**January 15, 2026 release.** NVIDIA researchers Rajarshi Roy, Jonathan Raiman, Sang-gil Lee, Teodor-Dumitru Ene, Robert Kirby, Sungwon Kim, Jaehyeon Kim and Bryan Catanzaro add text-role and audio-voice prompts to Moshi. Agent text/audio are generated while user audio continues. This establishes native incremental speech modeling, not durable relationship memory or proof of arbitrary deliberative reasoning at every word. The paper's **released** checkpoint table reports **170 ms smooth-turn and 240 ms interruption latency**; its main experimental table reports **70 ms and 400 ms**. They are different results and must not be combined. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06053v1), [dated NVIDIA project](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/adlr/personaplex/).

The [model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/personaplex-7b-v1) declares English input/output, Linux, A100/H100 support and A100 80GB test hardware. Weights use NVIDIA Open Model terms plus upstream CC BY 4.0; [code](https://github.com/NVIDIA/personaplex) is MIT. ServiceDuplexBench still appears as forthcoming on the card. No Hindi/Hinglish enterprise result was established here. A later Azure GPU batch could test an English control; neither small parameter count nor an attractive benchmark justifies a persistent GPU service now.

## 7. Griffin / VideoFDB — updated attribution and stronger comparison discipline

**October 1, 2026 disclosure.** Tavus credits Ioannis Patras, Head of Research, Hassaan Raza and its research team. Griffin-Lite combines continuing perception/conversational decisions with incremental audio/video generation. Its decoder's 10 ms packets and 2,193× H100 throughput describe the codec, not the caller's response delay. The disclosure is a company technical/product report; it does not expose enough internals to certify continuous deliberative reasoning or a fixed word-level cognitive latency. No downloadable weights, artifact license or Azure self-hosting path was verified. [Official disclosure](https://www.tavus.io/griffin).

Amrita Mazumdar, Seonwook Park, Rajarshi Roy and colleagues at NVIDIA/David AI provide a separate **VideoFDB** evaluation: 237 dyadic clips with model-judge rubrics. Griffin-Lite's generation row is 3.83 overall, 62.8% timing alignment and 1,892 ms median latency; its perception row instead reports 3.73, 73.8% and 2,232 ms. These tracks must not be collapsed into a single latency or quality claim. [Benchmark, named authors and current tables](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/amri/projects/video-fdb/).

This extends October 9's finding: adopt continuous observation and explicit decision evidence as architectural goals; do not treat the product's human-identification study as our quality target. Our disclosed AI voice should be assessed on task success, intelligibility, respectful delivery and appropriate floor behavior.

## What “full duplex” and latency must mean in our evidence

| Capability or metric | What would establish it | What does not establish it |
|---|---|---|
| Simultaneous media transport | Incoming audio remains accepted while outgoing audio is played | A successful turn after playback finishes |
| Incremental input observation | Timestamped partial audio/text evidence arrives before input completion | A final transcript emitted after silence |
| Causal interaction adaptation | The system continues, shortens, yields or repairs in response to newly observed evidence while output is active | Unconditionally cancelling on every speech start |
| Native duplex speech modeling | A disclosed model architecture updates on simultaneous input/output streams | Browser WebRTC duplex transport alone |
| Incremental reasoning | Evidence that new input changes a reasoning/result state before the utterance ends, under controlled probes | Tokens or audio packets arriving quickly; a model's marketing description |
| First audible response | A captured acoustic onset, with a named starting event | First server delta, response-created event or codec frame duration |
| Useful-answer latency | Time from a defined user boundary to the first correct, substantive answer unit | An “okay” filler token |
| Word-level reaction latency | Aligned input word boundary, decision evidence and output change, each with uncertainty | A frame size converted into an asserted reasoning interval |

These are operational definitions for our experiment. Preserve separate generated, queued, played and cancelled states; never substitute server generation time for browser playout. An assistant-track capture measures audio delivered to the browser capture path, not the user's speaker/headphone acoustic output or subjective hearing.

## Exact bounded change recommended now

Implement `DeliveryProfileV1` as an evaluation-only contract attached to the existing Azure session configuration and frozen run manifest. Use `profileVersion`, `paceIntent`, `requestedSpeed`, `appliedSpeed`, `applicationStatus`, `source` and `effectiveFromTurnId`. Suggested initial mapping is `normal → 1.0`, `unhurried → 0.9`. **Keep `brief → 1.0`: brevity is a content-length instruction, not permission to speak faster.** An explicit caller request or reviewed preference may select an intent, but an inferred emotional label must not.

Microsoft documents GA `audio.output.speed`, default 1, bounded 0.25–1.5; its session reference states speed changes belong between model turns. Audio-behavior instructions are guidance rather than guaranteed execution. [Azure Realtime reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/rest/api/microsoft-foundry/azureopenai/realtime). Route updates through existing scheduler ordering, apply only after the active response ends, and record acknowledgment before describing the profile as applied. Late acknowledgments must remain tied to their intended turn. Unsupported/rejected/unknown states must remain visible; do not silently claim a speed change.

Do not add pitch, emotional tone, phoneme, exact pause or emphasis controls to the Azure wire schema without a documented supported API. Keep them `unsupported` in a provider capability map. Keep current voice, language instructions, VAD and cancellation behavior fixed during the first comparison. These are modest adapter/evidence changes that need no GPU, Docker, provider replacement or new service. Follow with the already proposed playback ledger; selective floor control remains a separate experiment.

## Scientific comparison protocol

**Hypothesis:** on an explicit “please speak a little slower” request, the 0.9 profile improves listener-rated pace fit compared with 1.0, without critical-word loss or unacceptable completion delay. Register this hypothesis, profile values and analysis before generating samples. This is a defect-finding pilot, not evidence of general superiority.

1. **Freeze matched inputs.** Use 12 short business scripts: four each in English, Hindi and intra-sentence Hinglish. Include Indian names, product names, rupee amounts, dates and digit strings. Each script has a verified target and explicit pace request. Generate each condition three times in fresh equivalent sessions: 12 × 2 × 3 = **72 outputs / 36 matched pairs**. Interleave condition order to reduce time/network confounding. Fixed target text is a controlled recitation task; log deviations rather than selecting only good takes. This output-focused stage does not measure microphone recognition.
2. **Bind evidence before scoring.** Each output needs the playable assistant-only recording, digest, complete capture boundaries, frozen provider/model/voice/configuration, requested/applied speed, scenario/repeat IDs, source input digest and causally ordered trace. Permission to retain evaluation audio and retention expiry must be explicit. Missing capture/acknowledgment is a failed or unavailable trial; count it in reliability denominators and do not assign an acoustic score. Store no raw customer material in Git.
3. **Blind listening.** Three fluent Hindi/Hinglish-capable raters evaluate every pair in counterbalanced A/B order, tie allowed, with condition/model identity hidden. Rate pace fit, intelligibility, naturalness and professional respect separately. A critical-entity task may display the target spelling; the ordinary listening task should not reveal the transcript. Preserve individual votes and disagreements. One rater is not three observations because they replay a clip.
4. **Measure the sound and completion.** Report articulation/speaking duration and pauses from manually reviewed boundaries; use same-text ratios within each language rather than compare English word counts to Hindi tokens. Measure exact critical-entity correctness independently. Report request-to-captured-output onset and full-answer completion separately; include capture uncertainty. Speed parameter 0.9 does not imply an exactly 10% measured rate reduction. Report latency p50/p90 with raw N; do not overinterpret tail percentiles from 36 trials per condition.
5. **Analyze at scenario level.** Report paired preference counts with ties, all failure counts and per-language results. Treat the three generations and raters as clustered within scenarios; do not call 108 votes 108 independent samples. A scenario-cluster bootstrap can describe uncertainty, but four scenarios per language cannot support a precise subgroup claim. Report every scenario and difference alongside any interval.
6. **Predeclare pilot advancement criteria.** Proposed engineering criteria: no new critical-entity omissions, no unresolved wrong-speed/late-update leak into another turn, captured evidence for every scored sample, median pace-fit preference favoring candidate overall without an obvious language-specific regression, and median full-answer duration no more than 20% above control. The 20% limit is a product tolerance, not a literature result. Failure or wide uncertainty means retain the control and refine the pilot; passing means advance to a larger test, not change the customer default automatically.
7. **Then test interaction separately.** Replay the October 9 12-scenario duplex packet (backchannel, corrective takeover, explicit stop and hesitation across three languages), first with the unchanged control policy. Add consented live exploratory calls separately. Rate stop cutoff, premature response, continued speech through backchannels, stale audible words, repair completeness and memory/source correctness. A speed win must not conceal an interruption regression.

## Feasibility and evidence boundary

The immediate work is TypeScript/browser evidence plumbing and offline fixture validation. Any later paid sample run must use the configured Azure route within the user's authorized spend scope. Existing Azure metadata and the October 9 paced-recognition observation are historical evidence, not proof that this profile is accepted or sounds better. A zero-call code test can verify mapping, bounds, turn ordering, missing acknowledgments and manifest integrity; it cannot verify acoustic improvement.

Later self-hosted comparisons need a separately scoped Azure GPU batch, pinned code/weights/license inventory, remote build and shutdown budget. No laptop Docker and no non-Azure paid fallback. Prefer a single English released PersonaPlex or MoshiRAG comparison only after the Azure packet is valid; the current noncommercial RL/CtrlSpeech weights and unverified PERSIST artifact rights are excluded from SaaS deployment. No new infrastructure is required to adopt their evaluation principles.
