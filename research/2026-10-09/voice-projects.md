# Voice OSS and research adoption review

**Research date:** 2026-10-09 (Asia/Kolkata)
**Boundary:** No provider call, credential use, dependency install, model download, cloud change, build or Docker action was performed. Azure is the only approved paid route; local Docker is forbidden on the employer laptop. Published measurements below are author-reported until reproduced on this product.

## Decision

Ranked practical adoptions:

1. **Evaluate Pipecat Smart Turn v3.2 as an optional acoustic end-of-turn sidecar, beginning on the public gateway.** It is the best fit for the product's open turn-taking gap: permissive, small, CPU-oriented, audio-native and explicitly covers Hindi and Marathi. Do not migrate the full runtime to Pipecat.
2. **Add a bounded output-delivery contract and use Indic Parler-TTS as a research comparison arm.** This makes pace, pitch, emphasis and professional delivery testable without turning them into inferred caller emotions. Keep Azure as the product voice until an Azure-hosted trial wins the product's own listening and latency gates.
3. **Keep LiveKit Agents as the preferred telephony/room-transport spike when telephone work begins.** Its Azure Realtime integration, WebRTC clients and agent lifecycle are strong, but adopting it now would replace a working direct-Azure path and add a media server, TURN, worker deployment and another live STT stream for its turn detector.

The concepts must remain separate:

| Layer | Evidence and scope | Current owner | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Textual emotional cue | Explicit transcript wording such as “I am confused” or “please stop” | `lib/voice/emotional.ts`, `relational-runtime/` | May change wording, pace instruction or stop behavior for the current call. It is not an emotion diagnosis. |
| Acoustic prosody | Audible rate, pauses, pitch, energy, emphasis and non-speech events in generated or received audio | `lib/voice/maya/delivery.ts`; future delivery/turn trace | Evaluate observable sound. Do not derive identity, trust, intent or permanent facts from it. |
| Relationship memory | Approved, contact-scoped preference or open commitment with source evidence | `lib/voice/relationship.ts` | Retrieval needs provenance, review, correction/revocation and contact isolation. A voice model or acoustic classifier must never write it. |

## 1. Pipecat Smart Turn v3.2 — adopt as a bounded experiment

[Smart Turn](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/smart-turn) is a native-audio end-of-turn classifier built on a Whisper Tiny encoder. Its official repository lists 23 languages including Hindi, Marathi and Bengali, an 8M-parameter model, 8 MB int8 and 32 MB fp32 ONNX weights, 16 kHz mono PCM input and an eight-second rolling context. It runs after a lightweight VAD finds silence. The project reports under 100 ms inference on most cloud CPUs; that is a sizing hypothesis, not a product latency result. Code, weights and training material are BSD-2-Clause; the [model card](https://huggingface.co/pipecat-ai/smart-turn-v3) identifies the same license. It does not publish product-specific Hindi/Hinglish accuracy.

**Module mapping.** Start where the server already receives audio: the public gateway created by `lib/public-agent/gateway` and launched by `scripts/voice-gateway.ts`. Add a provider-neutral record such as `complete | incomplete | timeout | unavailable`, probability, model/version digest and monotonic timestamps. Feed only the decision into `private-turn-scheduler.ts`-style ordering; retain Azure interruption and cancellation authority until the candidate is proven. Extend `contracts.ts`, `client-adapters.ts` and `diagnostics.ts` with speech-start/end, decision, response-start, first-audio, cancellation and playback-stop events. The private browser path sends media directly to Azure, so Smart Turn cannot cover it without a new PCM side channel or transport change; do not silently duplicate microphone audio.

**Evaluation gate.** Use consented first-party English/Hindi/Hinglish business audio: hesitation, fillers, enumeration, rupee/date/phone dictation, self-correction, listener backchannel, side speech, corrective takeover and explicit stop, in clean and noisy conditions. Freeze Azure semantic VAD as control. Report premature-response rate, end-of-turn delay, false completion, missed completion, false interruption, stop-to-playback-cutoff and p50/p95 model time by language/condition. Adopt only if it reduces premature responses without materially increasing p95 reply onset; missing events remain missing.

**Azure feasibility.** High for a research sidecar: small CPU ONNX service on Azure Container Apps or a VM, built remotely in ACR/CI. No GPU is indicated by the project. Keep raw audio ephemeral and region-aligned; persist only bounded trace evidence. Full Pipecat is BSD-2, but its [August 2026 provider audit](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/provider-watch-reports/issues/2) still found fresh Azure Realtime configuration failures, while Azure Voice Live support remained under active work. A wholesale framework migration adds risk without solving the immediate measurement gap.

## 2. Output-delivery contract plus Indic Parler-TTS — adopt the contract; trial the model

[Indic Parler-TTS](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-parler-tts) is a 0.9B, Apache-2.0 checkpoint supporting 20 Indic languages plus English, including Hindi and Marathi. It accepts transcript text separately from a descriptive caption controlling speaking rate, pitch, expressivity, voice quality and recording conditions. The gated repository requires accepting its access terms. Its card says Hindi emotion rendering exists but was not extensively tested; the ten officially emotion-tested languages do not include Hindi. The primary [RASMALAI / IndicParlerTTS paper](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/sankar25_interspeech.pdf) reports 13,000 hours across 23 Indian languages plus English and human/perceptual evaluations, but its aggregate results do not establish this product's Hinglish business quality or streaming latency.

**Module mapping.** Introduce a small `DeliveryCue` at the text-to-audio seam used by `prepareMayaSpeech`: `pace: brief|normal|unhurried`, optional `emphasis: low|normal`, `energy: calm|normal|positive`, and `source: operator-default|explicit-caller-wording|scenario`. Compile it separately per adapter. `emotional.ts` may set it only from explicit text evidence; `relationship.ts` may supply an approved communication preference, never an emotion. Preserve transcript text and AI disclosure. Avoid open-ended tags such as “angry” or fabricated laughs; do not let provider markup enter memory or tools.

**Evaluation gate.** Add audio-backed cases to the Voice Evidence Lab rather than scoring them from transcripts. On identical English/Hindi/Hinglish scripts, compare the current Azure voice with an Azure-hosted Indic Parler research arm. Score exact words and names/numbers/rupees, code-switch pronunciation, first-audio time, real-time factor, truncation behavior, cue adherence and blinded human ratings for professional naturalness, pace fit and proportionate emphasis. Require two Hindi/Hinglish-capable listeners and retain raw vote counts/disagreement. The model is a useful control even if it never ships.

**Azure feasibility.** Medium. The model has no hosted inference provider; CPU execution is possible, but a latency-sensitive trial should expect a Python/GPU service. [Azure Container Apps](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/container-apps/gpu-types) currently offers scale-to-zero T4 (16 GB) and A100 profiles in selected regions, with subscription quota; remote ACR builds comply with the laptop restriction. The model card gives no production memory or streaming SLA, so T4 fit, cold start, concurrency and cost must be measured before any deployment claim. Newer [Indic-Speak](https://huggingface.co/bodhan-ai/indic-speak) covers 22 Indian languages but uses a custom license requiring written approval to host it for third parties, so it is not a cleaner commercial adoption.

## 3. LiveKit Agents — conditional adoption for telephony and shared media

[LiveKit Agents](https://github.com/livekit/agents) and AgentsJS are Apache-2.0; the self-hosted LiveKit server is also Apache-2.0. The framework provides WebRTC clients, job dispatch, telephony, provider-neutral STT/LLM/TTS interfaces and test primitives. Official [Azure integration documentation](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/integrations/azure/) supports Azure OpenAI Realtime through `RealtimeModel.with_azure` in Python and Azure model use from Node.

The caveat is material. LiveKit's multilingual end-of-turn model includes Hindi and is CPU-sized, but its [model license](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/main/MODEL_LICENSE) permits use only with LiveKit Agents. With a Realtime speech-to-speech model it also requires a separate incremental STT stream because final Realtime transcripts arrive too late for the text detector; the [turn-detector guide](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/turns/turn-detector/) explicitly requires disabling the provider's turn detection to avoid conflict. That adds cost, latency and a second transcript authority.

**Module mapping.** If telephony becomes active, spike one released agent behind a `VoiceSessionPort` preserving today's `VoiceEvent`, tool authority, relationship snapshot, disclosure and `private-turn-scheduler` generation/cancellation semantics. Keep `browser-session.ts` and the Azure adapter as the control. Do not let LiveKit room metadata become customer identity or permission.

**Evaluation gate.** The same frozen calls must pass on both transports: transcript ordering, duplicate/late events, tool idempotency, explicit stop, barge-in cutoff, disconnect recovery, ten-minute limit, memory isolation and bounded diagnostics. Add TURN reachability and mobile-network tests. Telephony adoption requires end-to-end call evidence, not a room connection.

**Azure feasibility.** Feasible but operationally heavy. LiveKit's [self-hosted VM guidance](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/self-hosting/vm/) supports Azure-style cloud-init but requires a domain, TLS/TURN and public TCP/UDP ranges. Its [Kubernetes guidance](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/self-hosting/kubernetes/) says media nodes need host networking and does not support serverless or private clusters. Agents themselves run as containers and connect outbound. A compliant Azure design is therefore a dedicated Azure VM (or carefully designed AKS) for LiveKit media plus remotely built agent containers; managed LiveKit Cloud would be a new paid non-Azure service and is outside the current boundary.

## High-value watchlist, not current adoption

- **Fish Audio S2:** useful design reference for word-level acoustic/prosody tags and multi-turn synthesis. The [S2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08823) and [model card](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/s2-pro) list Hindi/Marathi among 80+ languages, but [official inference guidance](https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/blob/main/docs/en/inference.md) recommends at least 24 GB VRAM. The March 2026 [Fish Audio Research License](https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/blob/main/LICENSE) grants no commercial rights without a separate agreement, including internal business use. Do not integrate its code or weights into this commercial product.
- **Sesame CSM-1B:** Apache-2.0 checkpoint/code with a Llama 3.2 backbone and CUDA requirement. Its [model card](https://huggingface.co/sesame/csm-1b) says it is a speech generator, not a dialogue LLM, and non-English performance is likely poor. It is an English conversational-TTS research reference, not an Indian deployment candidate.
- **Moshi:** technically important full-duplex two-audio-stream architecture; Python/web code is MIT, Rust is Apache-2.0 and weights are CC-BY-4.0. The [released 8B model](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/moshiko-pytorch-bf16) is English-only, has no tools, and its card recommends research rather than professional duty. The original [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00037) reports 200 ms practical latency on an L4, which is not transferable to Azure/Hinglish. A September 2026 [study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19596) found Moshi and PersonaPlex mostly took the floor when addressed or given silence, but rarely self-initiated to correct false facts or warn about hazards. Full duplex does not replace explicit stop rules, tool authority or content checks.
- **MaAI/VAP:** valuable separation of turn-taking, backchannel timing and backchannel detection; MIT source and CPU support. Current released MaAI models cover English, Chinese and Japanese, not Hindi. Use its event vocabulary in evaluation, not its runtime.
- **IndicEmo:** Apache-2 benchmark tasks help separate “professional,” pace and expression from intelligibility across code-switched Indian speech, but it is a one-commit emerging benchmark, omits recordings and judge text, and requires provider access to regenerate. Reuse only its observable rubric ideas with first-party audio; do not cite its table as product or vendor proof.

The next implementation should therefore be a provider-neutral turn/delivery evidence contract plus a Smart Turn replay sidecar. It strengthens the existing Azure route and produces the evidence needed before changing transport or voice.
