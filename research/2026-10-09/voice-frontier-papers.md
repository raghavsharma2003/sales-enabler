# Voice frontier: acoustics, adaptation and interruption recovery

Research checked **2026-10-09**. This extends `voice-projects.md`; Smart Turn, Indic Parler, Fish S2, LiveKit and the original Moshi/CSM catalog are not repeated. Priority was publications from the preceding 90 days, with one older Hindi-capable synthesis family retained for practical comparison. No model, dependency, cloud resource, provider call, credential or application code was used or changed.

**Decision:** pursue concurrent perception, floor control and streamed generation—not a full-sentence wait loop. Build an audible-progress ledger, separate present acoustic evidence from relationship memory, and evaluate vocal steering with recorded audio. These changes can improve the existing Azure route without financing a new speech foundation model. Recent full-duplex research provides valuable mechanisms, but none establishes superior enterprise Hindi/Hinglish voice for this product. Results are author-reported except the independently authored VideoFDB comparison; none was reproduced in this review.

## Eight candidates, ranked by product relevance

### 1. Self-Listening / LoopSpeech — adopt its playback-grounding principle

**September 4, 2026.** The model interleaves caller audio, generated text and **already-played assistant audio**, addressing the gap between generated content and what the caller heard. AnchorSpeech measures whether interruption recovery identifies the last completed spoken item. The paper reports 73.0% accuracy versus 7.8% for its matched two-channel ablation, with similar stopping latency; it also reports a trade-off against conventional turn-management scores. Structured synthetic sequences and exclusion of partial-item cuts limit generalization to business conversations. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05592v1).

Memory is short-term acoustic playback history, not a customer relationship store. Emotion is not evaluated. The [official repository](https://github.com/FreedomIntelligence/LoopSpeech) still lists code, checkpoints and evaluation data as forthcoming and exposes no reusable artifact license. Its Qwen2.5-Omni-7B plus MOSS-TTS-Realtime architecture is therefore a research reference, not a downloadable production candidate. A fresh application-level implementation of playback accounting needs no GPU.

### 2. SteerDuplex / SteerBench — adopt separate acoustic and semantic rubrics

**September 11, 2026.** Moshi post-training adds instruction control over delivery, persona and interaction. SteerBench uses 390 spoken prompts with separate human-authored text/audio criteria; fixed reference audio anchors tone, rate and articulation. The authors report 65.10% audio-steering all-pass, but only 13.64% Audio MultiChallenge task all-pass. Its memory/revision/self-correction tasks reveal how far fluent speech remains from reliable long conversations. Reinforcement learning can improve yielding while producing incomplete answers; timing alone is unsafe as an optimization target. Evaluation uses model judges, human-validated references and explicitly acknowledges training-source overlap in some duplex tests. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12623v1).

The context is joint text/audio, capped at 300 seconds in training; it is not durable relational memory. Requested emotional delivery is tested acoustically, not inferred as the caller's internal state. The [official repository](https://github.com/Utkarsh4430/SteerDuplex) says benchmark, inference, training and checkpoints are coming soon. No commercial weight/code grant is established by that placeholder. Adopt the experimental design, not unreleased artifacts.

Two closely related September papers sharpen this distinction. [CharDuplex, September 28](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34461v1), adapts GLM-4-Voice to simultaneous streams and trains persona consistency using an interactive simulated user. Its SpeechRole-Eval evidence concerns prescribed character/history consistency, not durable customer memory or objectively correct emotion inference. No official reusable checkpoint/code license was verified. [TASTE2, September 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08956v1), aligns acoustic latents to text tokens and streams CosyVoice2 synthesis; despite incremental processing, deployed mean first-audio time is **2.701 seconds on two RTX A6000s**. Fast-rate control is promising; emotion control depends on training strategy. [Model code](https://github.com/GitYCC/TASTE-SpokenLM-2), [VoiceBot stack](https://github.com/GitYCC/TASTE-Voice-Bot) and [English/Chinese checkpoints](https://huggingface.co/collections/YC-Chen/taste2) exist, but a complete commercial license chain was not verified. Neither establishes Hindi capability or affordable Azure deployment.

### 3. EmoUpdate — prevent relationship history from biasing acoustic perception

**August 21, 2026.** On one counterfactual diagnostic, changing only a supplied prior emotion label reduced current-audio classification accuracy from 72.50% to 30.42%. EmoUpdate first estimates current acoustic evidence without historical beliefs, then applies a causal state filter. It evaluates four SpeechLMs on CREMA-D-Stream and HumDial-En, using labeled emotion trajectories rather than proof of real human inner states. English/acted or benchmark annotations do not validate Indian business callers. [Paper and reproducibility appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20769v1).

Memory is a distribution over prior labels. This is exactly the type of state that must not become permanent relationship truth. A reusable official implementation/checkpoint license was not verified; the paper's arXiv distribution license does not supply one. Adopt the separation and counterfactual test in our own code. Do not deploy an emotion classifier merely to reproduce these scores, or feed a previous “frustrated” label into the next acoustic observation.

### 4. Realtime-Venus — useful separation of live conversation and background work

**September 2026; paper 2609.13814.** Released 9B Audio/Omni models separate live interaction from a task harness, returning completed work to the originating conversation. The official implementation distinguishes task completion from spoken delivery. Its long-memory examples concern **video perception**, not verified longitudinal relationship understanding. English/Chinese are declared; Hindi/Hinglish quality and calibrated emotion perception are not established. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13814), [model card](https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/Realtime-Venus).

Code and model repository advertise Apache-2.0, with explicit upstream model/third-party terms to review. The [official repository](https://github.com/inclusionAI/Realtime-Venus) requires at least an A100 for its online demo; this is not the cheap default. Its Codex-backed demo is outside our Azure inference boundary. Reuse the conceptual result-delivery lifecycle behind our existing authorization and generation checks; do not install its external executor.

Related September 24 [AdaptDuplex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29217) exposes interaction decisions as control tokens, adjusts time windows and delegates slower reasoning. This is an architectural research direction, not an adapter-level capability available from Azure or a verified commercially deployable release in this review.

### 5. NVIDIA NemotronLabs VoiceChat 11B — strongest released systems comparison, limited Indian fit

**Weights August 3; system paper September 18, 2026.** It combines incremental user ASR, agent text, separate tool-call output and streaming speech. The card reports 448 ms smooth-turn latency, but FDB-v3 tool argument accuracy is 42.2% and end-to-end pass@1 only 33%. Tool-selection scores therefore cannot establish successful business actions. [Current model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-NemotronLabs-VoiceChat-11B), [system paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21967).

Current weights use **OpenMDW-1.1**, rather than the research-only license described in some earlier coverage. It permits broad use with notice/redistribution conditions, litigation termination and responsibility for other rights/consents. [Governing license](https://github.com/OpenMDW/OpenMDW/blob/main/1.1/LICENSE.OpenMDW-1.1). NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech code is Apache-2.0; packaged runtime components need their own inventory.

The supported integration is English, with ASCII-only prompts/tool responses and NVIDIA GPU/Linux deployment. No durable personal memory or Hindi emotional-delivery evidence is established. An Azure GPU batch comparison is conceivable; a warm 11B streaming service is not justified before the current Azure voice is measured.

### 6. MOSS-TTS v1.5 / Local Transformer v1.5 — older but concrete pronunciation control

**May 26 / June 18, 2026**, outside the recent window. Retained because these are released Apache-2.0 synthesis models with declared Hindi, code-switching, IPA pronunciation, duration control and explicit pauses. The 8B model and 4B Local Transformer are distinct from MOSS-TTS-Realtime; the latter's language support must not be inferred from the family table. [Official dated release notes](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS), [v1.5 card](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-TTS-v1.5).

Prompt/reference audio and preceding synthesis context support voice continuity; they are not customer memory. Punctuation/prosody and multilingual improvements are author claims, with no product-specific Hindi emotion or Hinglish pronunciation result. The useful comparison is exact names, acronyms, rupee amounts and clause pauses, not a generic “empathy” score.

Azure-only feasibility: a short GPU evaluation job using remotely built pinned code; 4B is the more plausible first trial, but runtime memory, codec overhead, first-audio time and quantization quality require measurement. Do not assume 8B BF16 plus codec/cache fits a 16 GB T4. Neither model warrants always-on cost yet.

### 7. Orato Hindi ASR v1 / Qwen3-ASR — relevant recognition arm with a rights blocker

**Card checked October 9; exact Orato release date not verified** because history is gated. This Qwen3-ASR fine-tune targets approximately 1,000 hours of Hindi/English/Hinglish calling audio. The card reports Lahaja WER improving from 25.09% to 18.68%, but its market table mixes published sources/normalization and even lists differing FLEURS values. It is not a controlled win over today's Azure. [Official card](https://huggingface.co/tryorato/orato-asr-hindi-v1).

Crucially, Apache-2.0 metadata coexists with explicit **internal/beta evaluation only**, pending training-data commercial-license and consent/PII review. Do not ship or request gated access for this task. No emotional or relational memory capability is involved.

A cleaner research control is the upstream Apache-2.0 [Qwen3-ASR-0.6B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-0.6B): Hindi is supported, streaming requires its vLLM backend, and streaming timestamps are unavailable. Its separate forced aligner's listed languages exclude Hindi. Evaluate English/Hindi/code-switched recognition and partial-hypothesis churn separately; small parameter count is not proof of low end-to-end latency.

### 8. Tavus Griffin / Griffin Lite — architectural target and bounded comparison

**October 2026 technical disclosure, checked October 9.** Tavus describes a continuous conversational controller plus streaming audiovisual generators: perception continues during speech and silence, decisions occur at sub-second intervals, and synthesis consumes incremental text/control rather than waiting for a full utterance. Tavec's **10 ms packets and 2,193× H100 decoder throughput are codec measurements**, not end-to-end response latency. Voice reference conditioning and temporal context do not establish durable relationship memory. [Official technical disclosure](https://www.tavus.io/griffin).

Independent **VideoFDB** currently gives Griffin Lite generation scores of 4.25 fluency, 4.40 dyadic affect, 2.83 nonverbal-cue appropriateness and 3.83 overall, with **62.8% timing alignment / 1,892 ms median latency**. These are rubric/model-judge results on audiovisual dyadic clips, not a Hindi enterprise listening trial or universal superiority evidence. [NVIDIA/David AI benchmark](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/amri/projects/video-fdb/).

Griffin Lite is classified as closed source by that benchmark. No downloadable weights, self-hosting rights or Azure-only deployment path were verified; no Tavus account/API is authorized. Adopt the continuous-control requirement and evaluate it on Azure before considering a provider change. A related offline expression method, [SEmoEdit, September 28](https://github.com/imxtx/SEmoEdit), offers MIT wrapper code, but its TTS backbones/weights retain separate licenses and it does not establish live Hinglish latency.

## Three concrete algorithm adoptions

1. **Track generated, scheduled, played and cancelled output separately.** Extend `lib/voice/contracts.ts` and `diagnostics.ts` with response/item IDs, monotonic playback boundaries, cancellation acknowledgement and confidence/source of evidence. In `browser-session.ts`, `private-turn-scheduler.ts` and the public gateway, keep a bounded ledger for “repeat that / continue / what amount did you say?”. Preserve Azure's existing clear/truncate authority; a playback-start event cannot prove a word boundary. Only use a last-completed clause when timing/alignment supports it; otherwise ask a short clarification. This is an application adaptation of Self-Listening, not the paper's trained audio-feedback model. Test late response creation, cancelled but generated text, interrupted lists and mid-number cuts.

2. **Compile present evidence into bounded delivery decisions.** At `emotional.ts` / `relational-runtime/index.ts`, keep explicit caller wording, observable acoustic measurements and approved relationship preferences as separate inputs with expiry. A future acoustic observer receives current audio without earlier emotion labels. At `maya/delivery.ts`, compile a small pace/pause/emphasis contract per provider; expose unsupported capabilities instead of silently substituting generic style prompts. Keep `relationship.ts` unable to persist acoustic guesses. In `speech-vocabulary.ts`, retain spelling hints as ASR guidance; add a separate reviewed pronunciation representation for TTS only after the adapter supports it. Counterfactual tests keep identical audio fixed while changing historical labels and verify unchanged observations.

3. **Evaluate interaction intent and complete audible answers jointly.** Extend `components/voice-evidence-lab.tsx` beyond its existing transcript-only assessment with separate audio/trace attachments and human listening records. Measure continue/adapt/yield decisions, completed information and playback recovery, alongside delay. September's [Continue, Adapt, or Yield](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13117) and [TACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27372) motivate this richer rubric; do not copy inferred per-speaker psychological profiles into customer memory. Include “haan/achha” listener feedback, correction, explicit stop, hesitation and a requested slower pace. Audio-backed delivery rubrics must remain separate from quoted-text correctness.

**Current architectural gap:** `private-turn-scheduler.ts` waits for committed audio turns and their final transcripts before `response.create`. That protects ordered relational guidance, but is not Griffin-style continuously modelled floor control. A provider-neutral sidecar can observe partial audio and propose hold/yield/backchannel decisions while media continues; it cannot make Azure internally think ahead or synthesize from unavailable partial output. Keep response/action authorization on committed evidence. Evaluate a feature-gated path separating the fast interaction controller from slower relational/tool updates, with generation IDs preventing stale output. Do not replace the wait with speculative speech or tool execution on unstable ASR.

## Cheapest credible Azure evaluation path

First implement/inspect the ledger and deterministic replay cases on ordinary CPU resources; no new model serving is required. Freeze the current Azure voice/configuration as the control. Use a **pilot** of 30 consented scenarios per English/Hindi/Hinglish condition, at least three repeats and two fluent blinded listeners; this is a defect-finding pilot, not a superiority study. Cover ten-minute calls with preference revisions, delayed recall, self-correction, names/numbers, interruption recovery and background noise. Retain disagreement and missing evidence.

Report p50/p95 first-audio and stop-to-cutoff, premature-response/false-yield rates, heard-content recovery, exact critical-entity accuracy, speaking-rate adherence, naturalness and completion. Compare requested calm/unhurried delivery against what listeners actually hear. Never let an emotion-recognition score stand in for helpfulness or respectful behavior.

Only then authorize a time-boxed, single Azure GPU **offline** comparison arm: upstream Qwen ASR for recognition or 4B MOSS for synthesis. Build remotely, never with laptop Docker; shut down between batches. Record GPU active/idle seconds, cold-start/model-load time, achieved concurrency, cost per completed audio minute and failure rate before sizing any service. A100-scale Venus/VoiceChat trials come later. Costs, region/quota availability and grant applicability remain unverified; no deployment or provider purchase occurred.
