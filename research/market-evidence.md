# Market evidence: company brain, CRM agents, command centre and voice

**Research cut-off:** 21 September 2026. **Method:** primary vendor product, pricing, help and customer-story pages were checked on that date. A customer-story number is a vendor-published claim, not an independently audited benchmark; it is useful evidence of where buyers will pay and what a credible pilot can measure. Product/pricing pages change, so use the linked page rather than this memo to quote a customer.

## Decision readout

There is evidence of category demand for narrow, measurable agent work: vendors sell support resolutions, lead qualification, rep preparation, routing and routine voice calls. That does **not** establish demand, willingness to pay, or a product advantage for a new entrant. Incumbents already sell each component, often inside the customer system of record. A product hypothesis worth testing is a trustworthy operating layer for one narrowly defined business motion: a continuously maintained relationship/context graph, explicit permissions and approvals, observable agent runs, and a closed feedback loop that ties an action to a commercial or service outcome.

“Emotional intelligence” can improve the interaction, especially in sensitive support, but it is a model/API capability and a quality bar, not a moat. The hard product is knowing **what is true about this company and person now**, what the agent may do, when it must escalate, and proving the result after hand-off.

## What the evidence supports—and what it does not

**Category demand supported by the evidence.** Buyers of the cited products pay for a completed support resolution, qualified/disqualified lead, scheduled/routed next step, or contained voice interaction. Public pricing frequently uses outcomes or actions rather than seats: Salesforce ($0.10 action/$2 conversation), HubSpot ($0.50 resolution), Intercom ($0.99 resolution/$9.99 qualification) and Gorgias (automation fee on top of relevant ticket billing). This supports testing a tightly defined outcome for a new product; it does not confirm a commercial path, price point or outcome definition for that product.

**Not proven by the claims.** Vendor stories do not establish a universal automation rate, CSAT improvement, unit economics or causal revenue lift. They vary by channel, task complexity, data quality, customer self-selection and escalation definition. A 70% “resolution” at an easy FAQ-only launch does not imply 70% on billing disputes, sales objections or hiring decisions. Quotes that cite a percentage should specify: eligible population; share where agent was involved; share fully completed without human; 72-hour/other recontact rule; accuracy/CSAT; errors/reversals; and incremental revenue or cost measured against a baseline.

**Pricing caveat.** “Outcome” does not mean a simple, all-in result price. Salesforce meters individual actions and Voice actions; HubSpot requires qualifying product subscription and has included/expiring credits; Gorgias can bill ticket **and** automated interaction; Intercom treats a configured procedure handoff as a billable outcome; and voice stacks layer platform, model, voice, telecom, compliance and concurrency costs. Model/telephony usage, premium support and implementation are material at scale.

## Market-derived product hypotheses to test

1. **A verifiable company memory for one motion.** Test whether buyers value a time-stamped, permissioned evidence trail across CRM, call/email, product/support and policy data for a high-value motion (for example, B2B inbound qualification-to-handoff, renewal-risk recovery, or candidate pipeline coordination). Existing systems advertise context; the unproven hypothesis is that operators will pay to see why an action was proposed and what evidence is stale or conflicting.
2. **Cross-functional handoff with a single owner.** Test whether a command surface that detects a revenue/service/hiring event, assigns a human owner, executes bounded tasks across systems and measures closure outperforms the CRM/helpdesk’s native workflow. Begin with read/propose/approve before autonomous write actions.
3. **Agent QA as the product, especially for voice.** Test demand for simulation, policy tests, jurisdiction/consent checks, hallucination/error review, escalation reasons, transfer quality and post-call outcome reconciliation. These may prove more valuable than a pleasant voice, but the research does not establish that yet.
4. **Outcome measurement the buyer can audit.** Treat vendor headline metrics as the starting point. Test a dashboard that separates involvement, completion, human handoff, recontact, reversal, customer sentiment and incremental business result, linking each claimed result to record evidence and a defined counterfactual where feasible.

They offer the full enquiry-to-enrolment record, admissions/application workflows, documents, WhatsApp, calling/IVR, automation, attribution and now AI voice. ### A narrow hypothesis to validate

A testable product hypothesis is **decision-quality coordination at high-friction transitions**, configured for each consultancy rather than hard-coded to one vertical:

* Build a verified, time-stamped “student decision brief” from the student’s aspiration, academics, test scores, finances, prior messages/calls, documents and changing destination/university rules. Show source, freshness, conflict and missing evidence instead of presenting a confident generic recommendation.
* Coordinate the multi-party work that follows: counsellor recommendation, document owner, applications team, university/partner response, finance/loan/forex or visa steps, plus student/parent consent. The product should identify the blocking fact and assign/prepare the next bounded action.
* Treat voice/WhatsApp as channels into that process. A voice agent may ask for a missing fact, explain an approved next step and create a follow-up, but it must not autonomously promise admission, eligibility, scholarship, visa success or financial terms.
* Give managers a command view based on **risk and evidence**, not activity count: candidates with expiring offers, contradictory eligibility data, missing consent/documents, partner response delays, counsellor hand-off gaps, and application decisions needing review.

This could map to a reusable core: a permissioned case/relationship graph, evidence provenance and freshness, configurable decision rules, human approvals, cross-system task orchestration and outcome reconciliation. A study-abroad configuration would supply entities (student, parent, programme, university, document, application, offer, visa), rules and workflows. Validate both the buyer pain and integration feasibility before assuming it can generalise to other relationship-intensive services or avoid CRM replacement.

### Pilot metrics that distinguish the product from existing CRM automation

Measure a cohort against the consultancy’s current CRM process: percentage of active applications with a complete evidence brief; median time from new evidence to correct owner/action; stalled application/offer recovery rate; document/eligibility error or rework rate; manual counsellor preparation time; student recontact after AI channel interaction; and completed application/enrolment quality by lead source. Keep a human-approved record of every recommendation/action. Do **not** call higher call connects, message opens or agent conversations a business outcome.

## Recommended initial boundary

Treat an integration-first command surface around **one measurable workflow and one buyer** as a hypothesis to evaluate against replacement, add-on and services-led alternatives. Retell/Vapi/ElevenLabs/Hume can be replaceable voice layers while the buyer keeps its CRM/helpdesk. Pricing should be tested rather than mandated: compare platform fee + usage, platform fee + outcome bonus, and an outcome-led model. In study abroad, long and multi-touch enrolment attribution makes a pure outcome price difficult; any outcome component needs an agreed attribution window, eligible cohort, treatment of human intervention, cancellation/deferral and a reconciliation process.

The earliest proof target should be operational rather than aspirational: a live cohort with a before/after baseline; a published eligibility rule; explicit human approval/escalation; and measurement of completion, recontact, error/reversal, CSAT/trust and revenue/cost. That evidence would inform whether a “company command centre” has a product and commercial case against established platforms.
