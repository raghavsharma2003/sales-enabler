# Full-duplex architecture decision — 9 October 2026

**Recommendation:** preserve Azure and current authority/ordering guarantees; separate continuous perception, tentative preparation, floor control, generation and heard-history. First implement stage-level instrumentation and speculative **instruction acknowledgement**, with exact final reconciliation. This is useful latency work, but does not itself deliver Griffin-like continuous conversational reasoning.

Scope: source inspection and public primary documentation, not provider execution, listening tests or latency measurement. This report changes no source code. The first increment requires no new paid provider, local GPU, Docker or deployment.

## What the research establishes

**Griffin.** Tavus’s October 1 announcement describes a continuous conversational controller, making sub-second decisions from incoming audiovisual context while output is running, coupled to streaming speech and video generators. The controller can hold, yield, acknowledge or revise; speech generation receives incremental content and controls rather than an entire finished response. Causal generation makes past observations available without future lookahead. This architecture addresses conversational overlap; an avatar or shorter silence timeout alone does not. Griffin-Lite is described as a restricted research preview. The reviewed announcement does not establish public model weights, a generally available replacement API, or compatibility with Azure. Its human-identification study is not our target: this product must disclose AI identity. [Tavus Griffin](https://www.tavus.io/griffin)

**Sparrow-2.** Tavus describes an audio encoder plus six-layer causal transformer jointly tracking semantic/prosodic cues, speaker identity, background events, interruptions and backchannels, including the agent’s speaking state. It operates at 10 ms frames; the reported roughly 7 ms processing of 80 ms audio is component inference overhead, not audible reply latency. Its reported interruption/endpoint recall is not a false-turn rate. This is a learned conversational-state model, not an ASR keyword list. Sparrow’s own speaking-state mechanism is described as semi-duplex; do not equate Sparrow alone with Griffin’s complete audiovisual system. [Technical report](https://www.tavus.io/blog/sparrow-2)

Tavus exposes `sparrow-2`, patience and interruptibility through its hosted conversational-flow configuration. Those configuration controls do not provide a downloadable equivalent for this app. [Tavus API documentation](https://docs.tavus.io/sections/conversational-video-interface/pal/conversational-flow)

**Similar architecture, different deployment.** Kyutai’s Moshi jointly models user and assistant audio as parallel streams, producing speech or silence without requiring explicit conversational turns; aligned text precedes audio generation. Its reported 160 ms theoretical/200 ms practical model latency is not a browser-to-ear guarantee or a Hinglish result. Treat it as architectural evidence, not a proposed laptop deployment. [Moshi paper](https://kyutai.org/Moshi.pdf)

**Independent evaluation.** NVIDIA VideoFDB evaluates 237 dyadic clips across 11 conversational dynamics, separating perception and generation. Griffin-Lite’s published perception timing is 73.8% TOR alignment/2,232 ms median; generation timing is 62.8%/1,892 ms. These event-window measures are not directly comparable with Sparrow frame inference, Moshi latency, or our post-utterance response delay. The benchmark demonstrates why timely appropriate behavior and completion matter alongside speed; it does not prove vendor superiority for Indian business calls. [VideoFDB](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/amri/projects/video-fdb/)

## Current repository and provider boundary

| Layer | Current behavior | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Private transport | `lib/voice/browser-session.ts` sends the microphone track continuously through WebRTC and plays the remote audio stream. | Listening transport already continues during output. This does not expose continuous semantic decisions to application code. |
| Private response control | `lib/voice/server.ts` selects `semantic_vad`, `eagerness: low`, `create_response: false`, `interrupt_response: true`. `private-turn-scheduler.ts` waits for all queued committed utterances’ completed ASR in speech order, publishes user events synchronously, then waits for the exact revisioned instructions in `session.updated`. | Final ASR and the guidance acknowledgement sit on the reply critical path. Provider cancellation, generation fences and tool serialization remain necessary. |
| Public gateway | `lib/public-agent/gateway.ts` handles continuous input, cancels on speech start, correlates ASR to microphone generations, awaits transcript/adaptation work and configuration acknowledgement, then reauthorizes before `response.create`. | Anonymous authority, nonce/limits and stale-generation suppression cannot be bypassed for speed. |
| Playback/history | Public audio items bind samples to responses; browser played-position reports bound truncation and deletion of unheard later items. Private interruption delegates playback/history truncation to WebRTC/provider behavior. | Generated text and response completion are not proof the caller heard everything. |
| Diagnostics | `lib/voice/diagnostics.ts` measures accepted user transcript/typed input to first generated-output event. | It excludes endpoint/ASR delay and does not measure acoustic output. |

Azure Realtime natively consumes audio and supports automatic response creation after turn detection; the repository deliberately disables that response trigger to apply current-turn policy first. `semantic_vad` decides utterance boundaries; it is not a published Griffin-style application controller. [Azure Realtime guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/openai/how-to/realtime-audio)

Voice Live is a **separate adapter/API**, not a switch already implemented here. Its WebRTC media path uses RTP and separate control events. Its multilingual semantic VAD documents Hindi and configurable filler handling; language support does not establish reliable within-sentence Hinglish floor control. [WebRTC contract](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/voice-live-webrtc), [Voice Live configuration](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/voice-live-how-to)

Crucially, Voice Live’s reference says transcription begins when the input buffer is committed and runs asynchronously from native response creation; its partial-transcription event describes a transcription request in progress. Therefore a `delta` event name alone does **not** establish that meaningful text arrives before the speaker finishes. Record ordering for the exact deployed model/API before enabling speculation from it. [Versioned event reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/voice-live-api-reference-2026-06-01-preview)

Azure Speech SDK separately documents `Recognizing` as revisable intermediate text during recognition and `Recognized` as final utterance text. A future parallel recognizer would require approved Azure resource/billing and audio routing; it adds a service rather than proving the current Realtime route already offers this contract. [Recognition timing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/get-speech-recognition-results)

## Target causal architecture

1. **Continuous listening:** timestamp bounded audio/VAD observations while output plays. Keep echo cancellation; do not imitate Sparrow by simply disabling filtering. Each observation belongs to a session epoch and input item, with explicit provider capability flags.
2. **Speculative understanding:** maintain revisable hypotheses independently from the authoritative transcript. Partial text may prepare a local context lookup or candidate pacing brief, but cannot persist facts, extract final results, execute tools or authorize commitments. Corrections replace the hypothesis; they do not append another user turn. Promote only after final reconciliation. If no pre-commit semantic observations exist, honestly expose that capability as unavailable.
3. **Floor control:** distinguish `listen/hold`, `backchannel`, `speak`, and `yield`. A hesitation keeps the user’s floor; a backchannel does not seize it; an explicit correction yields promptly. Apply hysteresis and a bounded acknowledgement cooldown to avoid speak/cancel oscillation. Lexical “haan/okay” alone cannot determine whether someone acknowledges or interrupts. Initially these proposed decisions are shadow observations; provider interruption retains authority.
4. **Streamed generation:** separate preparing content from permission to play it. A future incremental provider must support input revisions during output and bounded cancellation of queued audio. Stream short coherent clauses rather than accumulating an entire paragraph. Any intentional overlap requires a separately evaluated floor policy, not disabling barge-in globally.
5. **Actually played history:** retain generated, queued, played and truncated states separately. Provider/client clocks need explicit mapping. Feed future reasoning only the heard assistant prefix where known; mark uncertainty where word-aligned playback is unavailable. Never infer “heard” from `response.done` or transcript completion. Application evidence must reconcile with provider truncation too.

This requires causal revision at every layer: a new input generation invalidates tentative guidance, pending output and tool continuations, while already heard speech remains historical fact. Authority, tenant scope, consent, memory provenance and reviewed-action semantics remain independent of timing policy.

## First implementable increment

Start in the private scheduler; leave public authorization and playback ownership intact.

* Add bounded metadata-only timestamps for speech start/stop, commit, first partial, final ASR, guidance sent/acknowledged, response requested/created, first generated output and cancellation settled. Correlate internally by item/generation/revision; never persist raw audio, transcript deltas, instructions or provider payloads in diagnostic logs.
* After a speech start, **once prior response cancellation and tool work have drained**, prepare a candidate from the currently approved baseline and bounded behavior. Send its uniquely revisioned `session.update` while the user continues speaking. Keep only one update in flight; serialize superseding candidates and retain existing timeout/fail-closed behavior.
* Store candidate acknowledgement separately from permission to generate. A matching ACK during speech must never invoke `response.create`. It only marks that exact candidate ready.
* When every pending utterance is committed and finalized, publish them in speech order and run the existing synchronous behavior compilation. Reuse the acknowledged candidate only if its generation, baseline and final compiled behavior match exactly. Compare the full compiled content; retain the candidate's already acknowledged revision marker instead of allocating another. Otherwise issue a fresh uniquely revisioned update and wait normally.
* Invalidate candidates on newer speech, changed context/behavior, close/failure and tool-state changes. Preserve duplicate-event suppression, late-response cancellation, captured-transcript saving and the existing one-response ownership rules. Instrument match/reject counts and saved critical-path acknowledgement time.

This overlaps a mandatory round trip; it does not remove the ASR/floor boundary or promise a fixed millisecond saving. It needs no new inference call. Add adversarial scheduler tests for ACK-before-final-ASR, changed final negation/language, resumed speech, out-of-order transcripts, stale ACK, delayed cancellation, tool completion and close. A second increment can consume partial observations in shadow mode after capability evidence exists.

## Phase two: act before the sentence finishes

The required behavior is **understand during speech, acknowledge at appropriate moments, and revise while speaking**. Pre-ACK cannot satisfy it alone.

First measure partial-before-commit coverage and partial-to-final revision rate on the exact Azure route. If unavailable, fan out the same echo-cancelled microphone capture to an Azure Speech SDK continuous recognizer while keeping the native Realtime media stream open. Feed bounded PCM through the documented stream interface; associate revisable hypotheses with session/item epochs rather than treating each intermediate result as another turn. Do not replay assistant output into this lane. [Speech audio streams](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/how-to-use-audio-input-streams)

This requires a Speech-capable resource/region, permitted authentication, concurrent-session quota, budget and Hindi/English recognition calibration. Existing Azure Realtime credentials alone establish none of those. Discover resource metadata within the approved subscription, then perform a separately bounded capability probe; no credentials or resource calls were inspected here. Keep service credentials server-side. Additional Azure recognition/interpretation incurs usage even though it adds no new provider.

Stable partial clauses can drive bounded, cancellable semantic preparation and floor hypotheses before sentence end. Audio prosody/backchannel classification remains a separate capability gap; text-only heuristics cannot reproduce Sparrow. The generation adapter must demonstrate how it conditions streamed output on a partial snapshot while later input continues, owns cancellation, and reconciles unheard audio. Do not assume `response.create` can consume an uncommitted audio buffer. If the selected native API cannot expose those controls, a separate Azure streaming text/speech lane is an architectural alternative, with its additional latency and state synchronization cost measured. [Streaming synthesis guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/how-to-lower-speech-synthesis-latency)

Initially allow only evaluated acknowledgements/clarifications during overlap; consequential answers and actions retain final-evidence/authority gates. Require acoustic evidence of appropriate overlap, continued listening and successful mid-response repair before calling this full duplex.

## Evaluation and acceptance

Use identical consented/synthetic audio and pinned model/settings for baseline/candidate runs; separate private WebRTC and public WebSocket, devices, networks and English/Hindi/Hinglish. Include 1–8-second thinking pauses, “haan” backchannels, explicit “rukिए/actually” corrections, names/numbers, negation, code switching, noise and speech during tool waits.

Report sample count and **P50/P95** for user acoustic end → first audible assistant sample; endpoint delay; final-ASR delay; guidance ACK wait; response generation; browser playback; and interruption onset → last audible assistant sample. Browser/provider timestamps are proxy stages, not acoustic truth; use consented loopback/manual annotation for audible measurements and distinguish backchannel from substantive-response latency.

Measure false floor-taking per annotated hold opportunity, missed/false interruption rate, accepted-backchannel rate, correction-to-stable-reply latency, repeated cancel/resume oscillations, task completion, premature commitments and unheard-history references. Report language slices separately. Compare candidate-match rates and stage savings before making a latency claim.

Proposed acceptance: lower matched-turn acknowledgement wait and non-worse end-to-end P95, with no ordering/authority regressions and no increase in false turns, repair instability or completion failures. Set numerical budgets after the first acoustic baseline; current text/Listen & type checks cannot establish them. Full-duplex product success requires appropriate overlap and stable repair, not merely faster first sound.
