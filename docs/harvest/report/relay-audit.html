Relay works. It is the wrong product.

Codex built a careful prototype of one workflow: company data, a reviewed brain, an AI campaign plan, a browser voice call and reviewed learning. Every flow I ran completed against real Azure. But it has no CRM objects, no ERP, no lead generation and no phone calls, and half its code is voice research. The command centre needs a new spine.

Recommendation Rebuild the shell, data model and UI. Carry forward about 13 mechanisms.

Combine three things: Taxila's platform spine (model gateway, Conductor runtime, gated deploys, sim lab), CommandCentre's go-to-market engines (sequences, inbox, ICP gates), and the html-portfolio safety layer (one compiler, one gated reply door, predicate safety, honesty gate).

Five stacks, one product

Voice stacks

The harvest settles this one: four sibling projects measured realtime speech-to-speech and rejected it for the speaking role. It ran 41 to 53 words per turn, its audio cannot be checked before it is heard, and it costs about 3× more. Taxila's measured winner is a cascade. Azure Voice Live is the ear, our model writes the words, a gate checks them, then Azure TTS speaks.

Laws we inherit

These were each paid for in a sibling project. A change that breaks one is wrong unless it brings new measurement that supersedes the old.

  1. The model chooses; code enforces rules, state and grades.A model running everything broke hard rules in 15 of 72 runs (Taxila). "Deal won" or "invoice paid" is earned from evidence, never asserted by a model.
  2. Safety and privacy live in predicates and SQL, never in instructions.Privacy enforced by prompt leaked 57–98% of the time; the SQL predicate leaked 0 in 31,122 checks (Meera).
  3. Check every word before it is heard: voice is a cascade.Realtime models talk before a check can run, at about 3× the cost (Taxila, Meera, Vyakti).
  4. One prompt compiler and one gated reply door for every channel.A hand-built realtime prompt lost two safety blocks, and Telegram skipped all gates (Meera).
  5. Rules that must fire go last; sentence-shaped prompt text gets recited.The same rule fired 0/8 when buried mid-prompt and 8/8 when placed last; example quotes were recited 4 times in 5.
  6. Never report delivery from an attempt; every send goes through a durable outbox.Measured false "sent" reports and duplicate replays; for Azure email, the 202 response does not mean delivered.
  7. One send path that re-checks suppression and scope at send time.Unsubscribe rejected every real address, and quarantined leads could be re-armed (CommandCentre).
  8. ICP accuracy beats volume: pre-labelled lead pools lie.The "EU-strong" label was right about 34% of the time; guessed domains sent about 156 emails to strangers.
  9. A metric with no writer is a lie: show "not measured", never zero.The complaints count was hard-coded to 0, and page views were about 3× bot-inflated (CommandCentre).
  10. Any AI-triggered delete, send or spend needs the human's request verified in code.44 memory rows were deleted with no recovery (Maya).
  11. Metrics do not pick voices; blind listening tests with accent as its own axis do.Azure TTS won every metric and was still rejected by ear as "not Indian" (Meera).
  12. Build the real journey first, before the ledgers.Vyakti built thousands of green checks without ever running one signed-in journey.
For our next conversation

To discuss next

The synthesis proposes a build order. Treat it as a starting point for discussion, not a plan, because market research and strategy come first.

  1. Journey first. Sign in, create an org, import leads with AI mapping, qualify them against your ICP, draft a campaign from approved brain facts, send through Azure email to a test inbox, receive and classify the reply, update the CRM, and show the result on a dashboard where every number cites its source. All on Azure.
  2. Spine in parallel. A Neon schema with org_id on every table and migrations only, an audit trigger, a model gateway, a budget ledger, one compiler and reply gate, the Conductor worker, and canary deploys.
  3. Agents. A chat agent with CRM tools, then browser voice on the Voice Live cascade, then the phone leg once a carrier is chosen.
  4. Sim lab before tuning. Simulated buyers with hidden goals, outcomes decided in code, and a noise-floor baseline.
  5. ERP, starting with quotes and GST invoices. CommandCentre's data says closing was the bottleneck: 1 of 7,538 leads ever reached a commercial stage.
Who is the first buyer?

Still open. The product has no customers yet, and the market research will choose the beachhead. CarbonSettle is a separate business, so its data says nothing about this market.

How much "AI runs it" on day one?
